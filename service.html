Before you call

Quick Checks
You Can Try

Many problems have a simple cause. These checks are safe to do yourself. If they don't fix it, book a visit and we'll take it from there.

Safety first: switch off the heater's breaker before touching anything, and never open the heater's cover. Leave repairs inside the unit to our technicians.

No hot water at all
  1. Check the breaker for the heater. If it tripped, switch it back on once. If it trips again, leave it off and call us.
  2. Check that the heater's own switch or power light is on.
  3. For a storage heater, give it time: a tank takes 20 to 80 minutes to heat up from cold.
The instant heater turns on and off
  1. Open the tap a little more. Instant heaters need at least 2 liters a minute to stay on.
  2. Clean the shower head and the inlet filter. Scale and dirt cut the flow.
  3. If the water pressure in the building is low, a model with a built-in pump (like Gratek Aquarius) may suit you better.
Water is only warm
  1. Turn the temperature dial or thermostat up a step and wait a few minutes.
  2. With an instant heater, a slower flow gives hotter water.
  3. With a storage heater, you may be using more hot water than the tank holds. We can size a bigger one.
Water leaking from the heater
  1. Turn off the power to the heater first, then close its water supply valve.
  2. A small drip from the relief valve can be normal while heating. Steady leaking is not.
  3. Call our service team. Don't open the heater yourself.
The breaker keeps tripping
  1. Leave the heater switched off. Don't keep resetting the breaker.
  2. This usually means a worn element or a wiring fault. Our technicians will test it safely.
Preventive maintenance

A Yearly Check
Saves the Big Repair

Scale, worn anode rods and tired valves build up slowly. A maintenance visit catches them before they cost you a heater, or a hotel morning without hot water.

SaferValves and wiring tested, so the heater can't overheat.
CheaperA descaled element heats faster and uses less power.
LongerA fresh anode rod protects the tank from rusting through.
  • Heating element and thermostat tested
  • Safety and pressure relief valves checked
  • Anode rod checked, replaced when worn
  • Tank flushed and descaled
  • Wiring, breaker and earthing checked
  • Flow and temperature measured at the taps
  • Heat pump coils, fans and refrigerant checked
  • A written report of what we found
